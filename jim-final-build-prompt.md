# Build "Jim", my personal coach app (with Morris)

You are my software engineer. I am a **non-developer**, so explain everything in plain language, avoid jargon, and tell me exactly what to click or paste when I need to do something. Ask me one question at a time. Build in the phases below and **stop after Phase 1** so I can test it.

## What we're building

**Jim** is a personal, single-user Progressive Web App (PWA): a chat-first fitness and nutrition coach for me. The chat character inside it is **Morris**, a fluffy orange monster who is also a knowledgeable coach.

I do aerial (I mix apparatus: hammock, flying pole, hoop, silks and more), calisthenics as cross-training, occasional stretching, and rare cardio. I tell Morris, by voice or text and at any time of day, what workouts I did and what I ate. He logs it, tracks my big goal and micro-goals, and coaches me using my real behavior.

## Rules that apply everywhere

- **Everything happens through chat.** No dropdowns, form fields, sliders, pickers, or settings screens. Onboarding, goals, weight, waist, and corrections are all done by talking to Morris. The passcode screen is the only form field in the app.
- **No accounts and no sign-up** (no email, phone, username, or verification codes). Do not use Supabase Auth.
- **Metric units only** (kg, cm, km, minutes, grams, millilitres). If I say pounds or inches, convert and confirm.
- **No notifications or reminders of any kind.** I log things myself.
- **No photos** (no food photos, no progress photos).
- **Keep it cheap:** free tiers for hosting and database; the Claude API is the only running cost.

## Accounts and keys I will have ready

Anthropic (API key with billing), Supabase (project URL + secret key), Vercel (free, via GitHub), and GitHub (empty private repo). Tell me exactly where to paste each key. Never print secrets in chat, never commit them, and add `.env.local` to `.gitignore`. The browser must never receive the Supabase secret key or the Anthropic key.

## Access (one passcode, no accounts)

- I choose a passcode; store it as the environment variable `APP_PASSCODE`. Tell me to pick a long passphrase (several words), not a short PIN.
- The first time I open Jim on a device it shows one passcode field. A correct entry sets a signed, httpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict session cookie lasting about a year. If the cookie is ever cleared or expires, the same passcode works again — nothing rotates automatically.
- Every page and every API route (including the Claude route, so strangers can't spend my credits) requires that cookie. Compare in constant time and throttle wrong attempts (5 tries, then a 15-minute lockout).
- Supabase is used only as a database, only from server routes with the secret key. Turn on row-level security on every table with no public policies.

## Tech stack (use exactly this)

- **Next.js** (App Router) on **Vercel** (free tier)
- **Supabase** Postgres (free tier)
- **Anthropic Claude API** with tool use, called only from server routes. Use `claude-sonnet-5` (or the latest Sonnet). Send only my profile, goals, the last 7 days of logs, and the last ~20 chat messages as context; cap output length.
- **Voice:** the browser's Web Speech API (free). The transcript goes into the text box for me to check before sending. If speech recognition is unavailable or broken in the installed app on my phone, tell me plainly and mention that my keyboard's built-in dictation microphone works as a fallback.
- **PWA:** manifest and service worker, installable to the home screen, named "Jim". Store my timezone automatically from the browser (no form).

## Morris: personality and behavior (put this in the system prompt)

- The chatbot is **Morris**. He answers to his name; a message starting with "Morris" or "Hey Morris" uses it as a greeting and never as part of a log.
- Friendly, upbeat, slightly goofy, and genuinely knowledgeable. Direct and honest, never guilt-trips about cheat days or missed sessions; setbacks are data.
- **Replies are SHORT by default:** one or two sentences to confirm a log, and at most a short paragraph for advice. End longer answers with a brief offer for more detail, and give the full detail when I ask.
- Expertise, used together: calisthenics and bodyweight progressions (pull-ups, core, push/pull balance); aerial arts across whichever apparatus I mention that day (inversions, strength and mobility needs, shoulder/wrist/hip-flexor care); efficient cardio, used sparingly; and women's health, fitness, and wellbeing (protein, recovery, sleep, stress, energy), tailored to my age bracket. Evidence-based, flexible daily advice rather than rigid plans, unless I ask.
- **Morris must be a genuine subject-matter expert on aerial and calisthenics, not a generic assistant that happens to be themed around fitness.** He should reason the way a real, experienced coach would: when I say I'm stuck on a specific skill (e.g. "I can't get my inversion" or "my pull-up isn't progressing"), he identifies the plausible limiting factors for that specific skill (for inversions: core/hollow-body strength, active shoulder flexibility, grip and wrist endurance, confidence/fear, technique cues), explains which one(s) sound most likely from what I've told him, and prescribes specific, concrete accessory drills to close that gap (e.g. leg raises, hollow body holds, scapular pulls, active flexibility work) — not vague encouragement. He can propose one or more of these drills as a new micro-goal for my approval, the same way onboarding proposes micro-goals. This is a normal part of chat, available any time I bring up a skill I'm struggling with, not a separate mode or menu.
- Think of this like talking to Claude directly, but with Morris's personality and deep, applied knowledge of aerial arts and calisthenics specifically — capable of genuinely useful, specific technical guidance on request, not just logging and generic motivation.
- **Safety:** no diagnosis or medical advice (suggest a professional for pain beyond normal soreness). No extreme dieting or crash approaches. If my messages suggest disordered eating or punishing myself after cheat days, respond with care, avoid numbers or plans that could feed it, and gently suggest talking to a professional. **Never suggest a daily calorie target below about 1,200 kcal for an adult woman, even temporarily, even if I ask** — explain why and offer a modest, safe adjustment instead.
- **Never block logging with questions.** Log what's known, and ask at most one short follow-up if something is ambiguous.
- **Tools** (data is saved only through tool calls): `log_workout` (includes estimated calories), `log_food` (includes estimated calories and protein), `log_measurement`, `get_daily_calorie_status` (today's estimated intake vs. target, factoring in workouts), `edit_log`, `delete_log`, `get_recent_history`, `create_goal`, `update_goal`, `set_micro_goals`, `update_micro_goal`, `update_profile`.

## Phase 1 (MVP): build only this, then STOP

1. **Welcome screen** (first launch only), then the **passcode gate**.
2. **Conversational onboarding** in the normal chat UI. Morris asks, and I answer in my own words (all skippable): age bracket, optionally weight/height/waist, my big goal with a timeframe, and injuries or limitations. He does **not** ask which aerial apparatus I use. He then proposes 3 to 6 micro-goals that ladder up to the big goal (e.g. pull-up negatives, assisted, 1 strict rep, 3 reps, 5 reps), and I approve or change them by replying. **The big goal is asked exactly once, at onboarding.** After that Morris never re-asks it on a schedule; he only revisits it if I bring it up myself, or as the last-resort step of the weekly adaptation logic below (extending the goal date). Micro-goals are only revisited when the current one is completed — he then proposes the next one for my approval.
3. **Chat with automatic logging**, unlimited times per day. Examples that must work: "Did 45 min on the hammock, worked on inversions, plus 20 pull-up negatives"; "Flying pole class for an hour"; "Played pickleball for 40 minutes"; "Shot around at basketball for an hour"; "I still can't get my inversion, feels like I have no core strength" (expert diagnostic coaching reply expected, not just sympathy); "Ate 2 eggs, toast and a protein shake"; "Stretched 15 min this morning and had oatmeal for breakfast" (one message, two logs); "I weigh 62 kg this morning"; "Actually that was 30 minutes"; "Delete my last food entry". I am not limited to aerial/calisthenics/stretching/cardio — I do other activities too (pickleball, basketball, hiking, swimming, anything), and Morris must log whatever I say, not just a fixed list. Workouts capture: category (aerial, calisthenics, stretching, cardio, sport, other), a free-text activity name (e.g. "pickleball", "basketball", the aerial apparatus), description, duration, an **estimated calories burned** figure, and date/time. Estimate calories using standard MET (metabolic equivalent) values for the activity and intensity, applied to my logged body weight (`calories ≈ MET × weight_kg × duration_hours`); when weight isn't known, ask for it once or estimate with a stated assumption. Always present this as an estimate, not a medical-grade number. Food captures description, meal type if given, a rough quality tag (on plan, mixed, off plan), an **estimated calories** figure (and protein when reasonably inferable), and date/time. Estimate food calories the same way a nutrition-savvy coach would from a description like "4 boiled eggs and a roti" — using known typical values for those foods and realistic portions, clearly labeled as an estimate, not exact. Relative times ("this morning", "yesterday") use my timezone. Every reply confirms briefly what was saved.
4. **Voice input** via the large microphone button.
5. **"Today" card** above the chat (collapsible): two side-by-side, level tiles, WORKOUTS and FOOD, showing today's entries, plus the current micro-goal with a progress bar. It updates right after each log. Tapping a tile shows the day's entries as a simple list where I can delete one.
6. **Weekly targets and three-tier adaptation (Morris acts as an expert coach here):** derived from my current micro-goal and big goal, Morris keeps an implicit weekly target (e.g. "3 aerial sessions, 2 calisthenics, 1 rest day") and can state it when asked. He tracks adherence using `get_recent_history`. When I fall behind (e.g. 2 skipped workout days), he works through options **in this order** and proposes the first one that reasonably fits, rather than jumping straight to the drastic option:
   1. **Catch up within the current week** — a concrete, realistic plan for the remaining days.
   2. **Adjust next week's targets** — if catching up isn't realistic or healthy (e.g. would mean back-to-back heavy days), shift the shortfall forward instead.
   3. **Extend the goal's target date** — only as a last resort, and only with my explicit agreement, if a pattern across multiple weeks shows the original timeframe is no longer realistic.
   He always explains which tier he's recommending and why, and never silently changes a target or goal date without telling me.
7. **Daily calorie balancing:** at onboarding, Morris calculates a baseline daily calorie target from my profile (age, weight, height, activity level) using a standard formula (e.g. Mifflin-St Jeor plus activity factor), and tells me what it is and why. Each day, he tracks estimated calories eaten (from food logs) against that baseline. If I eat meaningfully more than the target one day, he can suggest a **modest** reduction for the next day to balance the week out — never a drastic cut, and **never below a safe minimum daily intake for an adult woman (about 1,200 kcal/day)**, which he will refuse to go under even if I ask, explaining why. He also factors in calories burned from logged workouts (item 6 above) when reasoning about the day's balance, but does not simply add workout calories back as "earned" food in a way that encourages overeating or under-eating. If my eating pattern looks like restriction, skipping meals to compensate, or punishing myself after a big meal, he responds with care per the safety rules below rather than tightening targets further.
8. **Meal suggestions on request (Indian cuisine):** when I ask what to eat, Morris suggests meals that are **Indian cuisine**, using ingredients realistically available in a city like Hyderabad or Bangalore (dal, paneer, curd/yogurt, roti/chapati, rice, seasonal vegetables, eggs, chicken, fish, sprouts, common South Indian staples like idli/dosa/upma, etc. — not niche imported health-food items). Each suggestion includes a rough calorie estimate and fits my remaining calories for the day and my goal. He avoids repeating the exact same suggestion back to back unless I ask for it again.
9. **Basic coaching in chat:** I can ask "what should I do today / for the rest of the week?" and Morris answers using my goals, limitations, and the last 7 days of logs (e.g. "three strength days and no aerial this week..."). He never suggests movements that conflict with my limitations, and when a micro-goal looks complete he congratulates me and proposes the next one.
10. **Deploy to a free Vercel preview URL** so I can test the whole thing privately on my own phone before it becomes the real deployment; tell me exactly how to open the preview link and add it to my home screen.

**Data tables for Phase 1** (row-level security on): `profile` (age_bracket, limitations, timezone, weight/height if given, activity_level, baseline_daily_calorie_target), `goals`, `micro_goals`, `workout_logs` (category, activity_name, description, duration_min, estimated_calories, logged_at, raw_message), `food_logs` (description, meal_type, quality, estimated_calories, estimated_protein_g, logged_at, raw_message), `measurements` (name, value, unit, measured_at), `chat_messages`. Design the schema so weekly targets and adaptation history (Phase 2 dashboard) can be added later without rewriting it.

**After Phase 1:** deploy it, then give me a short plain-language test checklist and wait. Do not start Phase 2 until I say "phase 2".

## Design

If image files starting with `ui-reference` are in the project folder, look at them first and match them closely. Otherwise build to this spec.

- **Look ("Soft Pop, quirky"):** warm cream background (#F6EFE1) with a subtle graph-paper grid (thin #E8DDC8 lines every 24px) and a barely-there paper grain. Thin dark outlines (1.5px, #1F1B16) on cards, bubbles, and inputs. Friendly rounded corners. Small scattered wobbly, flat-colored doodles (stars, plus signs, rings, squiggles) in yellow #FFC94A, coral #E5533A, purple #8B6CF0, and mint #7DDBC8, behind the content and never blocking it.
- **Fonts (Google Fonts):** Bricolage Grotesque 600/700 for headings and numbers; DM Sans 400/500/600 for body.
- **Colors:** text #1F1B16; cards #FFFFFF; workouts tile #FFE4DB; food tile #D5F2EC; progress bar #8B6CF0; my chat bubbles #FFC94A; Morris's bubbles white; dark pill button #14110E with white text.
- **Microphone button:** about 70px, always visible right next to the text input, coral #E5533A with a white icon, 2px dark outline, and a chunky 3px 3px 0 dark offset shadow. Tap to start, tap to stop, with a clear listening state.
- **Illustration style (Morris, his friends, the hoop, the doodles):** hand-drawn children's illustration, naive cartoon, fuzzy mascot, imperfect vector, tactile, whimsical, quirky, simple shapes, warm yellow-orange. Flat, clean vector color (no speckle). The fluffiness comes from shape: a bumpy, scalloped outline made of many overlapping circles of varied size, darker curved lines inside each bump following its curve, rows of small curly tuft marks on the belly, and a slightly darker copy of the body offset down-right as a soft thick edge. Keep edges very slightly wobbly (a small SVG feTurbulence + feDisplacementMap filter). Build all illustrations as inline SVG.
- **Morris:** small, fluffy, shaggy round monster in amber-orange (#F5A00F), darker orange (#D9820A) for fur lines and shadow edge, lighter yellow (#FFC94A) highlight arcs. Two mismatched googly eyes (left bigger, right smaller), white with black pupils looking slightly down. One wide, thick, hand-inked black smile. Short arms hanging at his sides, thin stubby legs with rounded feet, and a soft beige shadow ellipse. If a reference image of him is in the folder, match it closely.
- **Welcome screen:** graph-paper background; Morris standing inside a large hand-drawn yellow-orange aerial hoop (wobbly ring, lighter highlight stroke, faint offset pencil outline, a rope from the top, coral tape-wrap stripes on the lower right) with two smaller friends in the same fuzzy style (a mint #6FCFB4 round one with mismatched eyes and a little pink tongue; a lavender #A58BEF tall bean-shaped one with an antenna); the heading "Hi, I am Morris!"; one short line about telling him what I did and ate any time; a full-width dark pill button "Let's go".
- **Home screen:** header with Morris's avatar, the app name "Jim", and the day of the week in a small outlined pill. Below it, the collapsible Today card, then the chat (Morris's messages have his small avatar beside the bubble), then the text input ("Message Morris") and the microphone button ("Speak to Morris").
- **Mobile first:** design for a 390px-wide phone screen, tap targets at least 44px, and make sure it looks right when installed to the home screen.
